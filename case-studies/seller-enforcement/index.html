Case study 02 / IRIS Seller Enforcement

Building complex operational workflows

Internal seller-enforcement tools supporting workflows across the US, Canada, and Mexico.

EngagementWalmart via Randstad · 2022–2026
FocusMulti-market frontend
In this study01 / Context02 / My contribution03 / Engineering perspective04 / Delivery

The context

Internal enforcement work involves multiple actions and information sources: payment deactivation, reason-code catalogs, bulk actions, and enforcement history. The frontend needs to make those workflows available within the appropriate permissions and market context.

Permissions + market → Workflow + APIs → History + support

Conceptual view of the work, not a production architecture diagram.

My role
Owned frontend development for internal seller-enforcement workflows.

What I worked on

  • Delivered bulk actions, payment deactivation, reason-code catalogs, and enforcement history.
  • Integrated enforcement APIs and role-based permissions using React, TypeScript, Electrode, and TanStack Query.
  • Maintained Jest and React Testing Library coverage, managed feature flags, and provided recurring on-call support.
ReactTypeScriptTanStack Query

Engineering perspective

A workflow can be technically correct and still be difficult to operate. Permissions, history, and market-specific behavior make explicit states and clear action context especially important in operational tools.

What I delivered

Frontend workflows supporting three marketplaces, with automated tests, controlled releases, and production support.
